# B2 · Lesson 2 — Giving Opinions & Disagreeing Politely

## 🎯 Goal
Share your opinion in technical discussions and disagree **politely** — a skill that
makes you look senior and confident.

---

## 📖 Phrases

| English | Meaning (Hindi hint) | Example |
|---------|----------------------|---------|
| In my opinion… | मेरी राय में… | "In my opinion, we should refactor this first." |
| I'd suggest… | मैं सुझाव दूँगा… | "I'd suggest we add tests before merging." |
| I see your point, but… | मैं समझ रहा हूँ, लेकिन… | "I see your point, but it might be slow." |
| I'm not sure that's… | मुझे यकीन नहीं कि यह… | "I'm not sure that's the best approach." |
| What if we…? | अगर हम … करें तो? | "What if we split this into two services?" |
| That's a good idea | यह अच्छा विचार है | "That's a good idea, let's try it." |

---

## 💬 Dialogue — Disagreeing in a design meeting

> **Teammate:** I think we should put all the logic in one big service.
> **You:** I see your point — it's simpler to start with. But I'm a little concerned it
> might become hard to maintain later. What if we split it by responsibility instead?
> **Teammate:** Wouldn't that be more work?
> **You:** A bit more upfront, yes. But in my experience, it saves a lot of time when we
> add features. I'd suggest we at least separate the payment logic.
> **Teammate:** Okay, that's fair. Let's do that.

---

## 🔑 Key phrases to reuse

- **Soft opening:** "I see your point, but…" / "That makes sense, though I'd add…"
- **Your view:** "In my opinion…" / "In my experience…" / "I'd suggest…"
- **Proposing:** "What if we…?" / "How about we…?"
- **Agreeing:** "That's fair." / "Good point." / "I'm on board with that."

---

## 🗣️ Say it out loud (drill)

Someone proposes putting all code in one class. Disagree politely, out loud:
1. Acknowledge: "I see your point, but…"
2. Give your concern: "I'm a bit concerned that…"
3. Propose an alternative: "What if we…?"

Record it. Did you disagree **without** sounding rude or unsure?

---

## ⚠️ Watch out (common Hindi-speaker mistake)

- ❌ "No no, that is wrong." *(too direct — can sound rude)*
- ✅ "I see it a little differently." / "I'm not sure that'll work because…"
- **Why:** Direct "you're wrong" feels harsh in English work culture. Soften it; it makes
  you sound more senior, not weaker.

- ❌ "According to me…"
- ✅ "In my opinion…" / "The way I see it…"
- **Why:** "According to me" is direct translation and uncommon. "According to" is used for
  *other sources* ("according to the docs").
