# B1 · Lesson 3 — Asking for Help & Clarification

## 🎯 Goal
Ask for help or clarification politely and confidently — without feeling you're
"disturbing" anyone. This is a key skill many developers avoid, and it slows them down.

---

## 📖 Words & phrases

| English | Meaning (Hindi hint) | Example |
|---------|----------------------|---------|
| Do you have a minute? | क्या आपके पास एक मिनट है? | "Hey, do you have a minute?" |
| I'm a bit stuck on… | मैं … पर थोड़ा अटका हूँ | "I'm a bit stuck on this bug." |
| Could you point me to…? | क्या आप मुझे … बता सकते हैं? | "Could you point me to the docs?" |
| Just to confirm… | बस पुष्टि के लिए… | "Just to confirm, you want X, right?" |
| Does that make sense? | क्या यह समझ आया? | "I'll do it by Friday — does that make sense?" |
| If I understand correctly… | अगर मैं सही समझा हूँ तो… | "If I understand correctly, you need the GET endpoint first." |

---

## 💬 Dialogue — Asking a senior for help

> **You:** Hey Rahul, do you have a minute?
> **Rahul:** Sure, what's up?
> **You:** I'm a bit stuck on the deployment. The build passes locally but fails on the
> server. Could you point me in the right direction?
> **Rahul:** Did you check the environment variables?
> **You:** Just to confirm — you mean the ones in the config file, right?
> **Rahul:** Exactly.
> **You:** Got it, I'll check those. Thanks a lot!

---

## 🔑 Key phrases to reuse

- "Do you have a minute?" / "Is this a good time?"
- "I'm a bit stuck on … — could you help me out?"
- "Just to confirm, you mean …, right?"
- "If I understand correctly, …"
- "That makes sense, thanks!" / "Got it, thank you."

---

## 🗣️ Say it out loud (drill)

Imagine you're stuck on a real bug. Out loud:
1. Ask politely for a minute of someone's time.
2. Explain what you're stuck on in ONE sentence.
3. Confirm their suggestion: "Just to confirm, you mean …, right?"

Record it. Did you sound confident (not apologetic 5 times)?

---

## ⚠️ Watch out (common Hindi-speaker mistake)

- ❌ "Can you explain me this?"
- ✅ "Can you explain this **to me**?"
- **Why:** "Explain" takes "**to** someone": explain *something* **to** *someone*.

- ❌ "I have a doubt."
- ✅ "I have a **question**." / "I'm not sure about…"
- **Why:** "Doubt" is very common in Indian English but to most speakers "doubt" means
  *suspicion/not believing*. For asking something, say "**question**".

- ❌ "Please do the needful." *(sounds old-fashioned/unclear)*
- ✅ "Could you please **look into this**?" / "Could you **take care of this**?"
- **Why:** State the actual action you need.
