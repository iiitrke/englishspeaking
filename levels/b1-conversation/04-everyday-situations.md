# B1 · Lesson 4 — Everyday Situations (Shopping, Restaurant, Travel)

## 🎯 Goal
Handle common daily situations in English. General fluency helps your work English too —
the more you speak, the more natural everything becomes.

---

## 🛒 Shopping

> **You:** Excuse me, do you have this in a larger size?
> **Staff:** Let me check… yes, we have medium and large.
> **You:** Could I try the large one?
> **Staff:** Of course, the trial room is on the right.
> **You:** Thanks. How much is this one?
> **Staff:** It's 1,200 rupees.
> **You:** Okay, I'll take it. Can I pay by card?

**Key phrases:** "Do you have this in…?" · "Could I try…?" · "How much is this?" · "I'll take it." · "Can I pay by card?"

---

## 🍽️ Restaurant

> **Waiter:** Are you ready to order?
> **You:** Almost. What would you recommend?
> **Waiter:** The paneer tikka is very popular.
> **You:** Sounds good. I'll have that, and a plain naan, please.
> **Waiter:** Anything to drink?
> **You:** Just water for now, thanks. Could we get the bill when you have a moment?

**Key phrases:** "What would you recommend?" · "I'll have…" · "…please." · "Could we get the bill?"

---

## ✈️ Travel

> **You:** Hi, I have a booking under the name Sanjay.
> **Receptionist:** Let me check… yes, a deluxe room for two nights.
> **You:** That's right. What time is check-out?
> **Receptionist:** Check-out is at 11 a.m.
> **You:** Great. Is breakfast included?
> **Receptionist:** Yes, from 7 to 10 in the morning.

**Key phrases:** "I have a booking under…" · "What time is…?" · "Is … included?"

---

## 🗣️ Say it out loud (drill)

Role-play all three out loud. Play **both** people. Change the details to be about you.

---

## ⚠️ Watch out (common Hindi-speaker mistake)

- ❌ "I want to purchase this one, no?" / "…, isn't it?"
- ✅ "I'd like to buy this one." (Drop the extra "no?" / "isn't it?" tag.)
- **Why:** Adding "no?" / "isn't it?" to everything is a Hindi habit ("ना?"). Use it rarely.

- ❌ "Give me one coffee." *(sounds like an order/command)*
- ✅ "Could I **get** a coffee, please?" / "I'll **have** a coffee, please."
- **Why:** "Could I…please" is polite; "Give me" sounds rude in English.
